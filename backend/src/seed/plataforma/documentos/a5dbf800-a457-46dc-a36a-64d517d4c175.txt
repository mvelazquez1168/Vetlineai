Chapter 1 — Introduction

1.1 Purpose

The Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base (BIKB) defines the standard architecture for representing breed-specific veterinary knowledge within the Internal Medicine Clinical Intelligence System (ICIS).

Its purpose is to provide the AI engines with structured, evidence-based information regarding breed characteristics that influence clinical reasoning, disease predisposition, diagnostic prioritization, interpretation of clinical findings, and knowledge discovery.

The Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base shall not function as a static encyclopedia. Instead, it shall operate as an active knowledge domain consumed by multiple clinical intelligence engines throughout the platform.

1.2 Vision

The long-term vision of the Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base is to enable the ICIS to reason in a manner similar to an experienced veterinary gastroenterologist.

When a clinician identifies the breed of a patient, years of accumulated medical knowledge are immediately incorporated into the diagnostic reasoning process. The ICIS shall emulate this capability by automatically loading breed-specific intelligence before interpreting laboratory tests, imaging studies, endoscopic findings, histopathology, or diagnostic hypotheses.

Breed knowledge shall therefore become part of the clinical context rather than a simple demographic attribute.

1.3 Scope

The Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base shall maintain structured knowledge describing canine and feline breeds, including but not limited to:

-   Breed identification

-   Morphological characteristics

-   Physiological characteristics

-   Genetic characteristics

-   Gastrointestinal predispositions

-   Hepatic disorders

-   Pancreatic disorders

-   Nutritional considerations

-   Immune-mediated diseases

-   Congenital disorders

-   Typical laboratory alterations

-   Typical imaging findings

-   Typical endoscopic findings

-   Histopathologic patterns

-   Pharmacologic considerations

-   Prognostic factors

-   Published scientific evidence

The knowledge base shall initially support domestic dogs and cats, while remaining extensible to additional companion animal species in future releases.

1.4 Objectives

The Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base has the following objectives:

Clinical Objectives

-   Support diagnostic reasoning.

-   Improve differential diagnosis ranking.

-   Increase diagnostic confidence.

-   Reduce overlooked breed-associated diseases.

-   Provide explainable breed-based clinical insights.

Knowledge Objectives

-   Centralize breed-specific medical knowledge.

-   Standardize terminology.

-   Preserve scientific references.

-   Support semantic search.

-   Enable longitudinal knowledge enrichment.

AI Objectives

-   Provide Bayesian prior probabilities.

-   Supply contextual information to AI engines.

-   Enable similarity analysis.

-   Improve pattern recognition.

-   Support explainable artificial intelligence.

1.5 Guiding Principles

The design of the Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base shall follow the following principles:

Scientific Evidence First

Every medical assertion shall be traceable to scientific evidence.

Explainability

Every recommendation generated by the AI must be explainable through explicit breed knowledge.

Separation of Knowledge and Reasoning

The Knowledge Base stores veterinary knowledge.

AI Engines consume that knowledge.

The Knowledge Base shall never execute reasoning by itself.

Continuous Evolution

Scientific knowledge changes.

The Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base shall support continuous updates without impacting system architecture.

Traceability

Every clinical recommendation shall be traceable back to:

-   literature

-   guideline

-   consensus

-   expert opinion

-   published evidence

Chapter 2 — Knowledge Domain Architecture

2.1 Purpose

The Knowledge Domain Architecture defines the internal structure of the Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base.

Its purpose is to provide a standardized semantic framework for representing breed-specific veterinary knowledge that can be consumed consistently by every AI engine within the Internal Medicine Clinical Intelligence System.

The architecture separates knowledge representation from clinical reasoning, allowing the knowledge base to evolve independently of the reasoning algorithms.

2.2 Architectural Principles

The Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base shall be governed by the following architectural principles.

Principle 1 — Knowledge is Structured

Knowledge shall never be stored as free text.

Every concept shall be represented as a structured entity with defined semantics.

Examples include:

-   Breed

-   Disease

-   Organ

-   Biomarker

-   Laboratory Test

-   Imaging Finding

-   Histopathologic Pattern

-   Drug

-   Genetic Mutation

-   Nutritional Requirement

Principle 2 — Knowledge is Evidence-Based

Every clinical assertion shall reference its scientific evidence.

No statement shall exist without provenance.

Each assertion shall include:

-   Source

-   Publication

-   Evidence Level

-   Confidence Score

-   Date of Review

-   Reviewer

Principle 3 — Knowledge is Explainable

Every AI recommendation generated from breed intelligence shall be explainable.

The system shall always identify:

-   Why a breed characteristic was considered.

-   Which scientific evidence supports it.

-   How it influenced the diagnostic reasoning.

-   Whether contradictory evidence exists.

Principle 4 — Knowledge is Modular

Knowledge shall be organized into reusable domains.

The Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base shall not duplicate information stored in other Knowledge Domains.

Cross-domain references shall be established through semantic relationships.

Principle 5 — Knowledge Evolves

Scientific evidence changes over time.

The architecture shall support:

-   Versioning

-   Deprecation

-   Replacement

-   Evidence updates

-   Historical traceability

without affecting downstream AI engines.

Every Knowledge Object shall include a Confidence Score.

Confidence reflects the reliability of the knowledge.

It is derived from:

-   Evidence quality.

-   Number of independent publications.

-   Consensus support.

-   Clinical validation.

-   Publication recency.

Confidence shall never be inferred solely from AI.

2.16 AI Consumption Contract

All AI engines interacting with the Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base shall adhere to the following rules:

-   Consume only published Knowledge Objects.

-   Preserve semantic relationships.

-   Avoid deterministic conclusions based solely on breed predisposition.

-   Recalculate diagnostic priors dynamically as new patient evidence becomes available.

Chapter 3 — Breed Ontology

The Breed Ontology defines the formal semantic structure used to represent canine and feline breeds within the Internal Medicine Clinical Intelligence System.

Its purpose is to establish a standardized vocabulary that enables all AI engines to reason consistently about breed-related knowledge, inheritance, disease predisposition, physiology, anatomy and clinical characteristics.

The ontology shall constitute the authoritative semantic model for all breed information stored within the Knowledge Intelligence Layer.

3.2 Design Goals

3.20 AI Prompt Contract

When a patient's breed is identified, the AI shall:

1.  Resolve the breed to its canonical ontology object.

2.  Load inherited knowledge from the species and breed group.

3.  Load breed-specific characteristics and overrides.

4.  Retrieve associated diseases, biomarkers, laboratory patterns, imaging findings, endoscopic patterns and histopathologic features.

5.  Calculate breed-adjusted prior probabilities for diagnostic hypotheses.

6.  Record all ontology-derived relationships in the Clinical Evidence Graph.

7.  Explain explicitly how breed knowledge influenced the diagnostic reasoning.

The AI shall never treat breed information as diagnostic evidence by itself. Breed information modifies prior probability, but definitive conclusions shall always depend on patient-specific clinical evidence.

Chapter 5 — Clinical Intelligence Model

5.1 Purpose

The Clinical Intelligence Model defines how breed-specific knowledge influences clinical reasoning within the Internal Medicine Clinical Intelligence System (ICIS).

Its purpose is not to diagnose disease.

Its purpose is to provide contextual intelligence that dynamically modifies the interpretation of patient-specific clinical evidence.

Breed information shall therefore be considered a context modifier, never a diagnostic conclusion.

5.2 Clinical Reasoning Philosophy

The ICIS follows the same reasoning sequence used by experienced veterinary specialists.

Patient Identity

↓

Breed Intelligence

↓

Clinical History

↓

Physical Examination

↓

Laboratory Evidence

↓

Diagnostic Imaging

↓

Endoscopy

↓

Histopathology

↓

Diagnostic Correlation

Breed knowledge is evaluated before laboratory interpretation, but after patient identification.

5.3 Role of Breed Intelligence

Breed intelligence contributes to:

-   Diagnostic prioritization

-   Differential diagnosis ranking

-   Interpretation of laboratory abnormalities

-   Interpretation of imaging findings

-   Interpretation of histopathology

-   Selection of confirmatory tests

-   Clinical report generation

-   Case similarity analysis

-   Pattern recognition

Breed intelligence shall never replace objective evidence.

5.4 Clinical Knowledge Categories

Every Breed Knowledge Object shall organize its knowledge into standardized clinical domains.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Domain                       Purpose
  ---------------------------- ------------------------------------------
  Disease Predisposition       Relative disease risk

  Age Susceptibility           Expected age of onset

  Sex Predisposition           Male/Female influence

  Clinical Presentation        Typical signs and symptoms

  Laboratory Profile           Expected laboratory abnormalities

  Imaging Profile              Typical imaging findings

  Endoscopic Profile           Expected endoscopic lesions

  Histopathology Profile       Typical microscopic findings

  Therapeutic Response         Common treatment response

  Prognostic Factors           Outcome modifiers
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

5.16 AI Prompt Contract

When a patient's breed is identified, the AI shall:

1.  Load the corresponding Breed Knowledge Object.

2.  Generate the Prior Probability Profile.

3.  Identify expected clinical patterns.

4.  Apply risk modifiers.

5.  Compare patient evidence with expected breed-specific findings.

6.  Continuously update diagnostic probabilities as new evidence is incorporated.

7.  Explain every change in confidence using explicit clinical evidence.

8.  Record all breed-derived inferences in the Clinical Evidence Graph (CC-250).

The AI shall never:

-   Diagnose a disease solely because it is common in a breed.

-   Ignore contradictory evidence.

-   Inflate probabilities beyond what is supported by scientific evidence.

-   Conceal the role that breed intelligence played in the reasoning process.

5.18 Integration with Referral Threshold (CC-260)

El riesgo racial es uno de los insumos que consume CC-260 (Referral Threshold Engine, ICIS-ARCH-001) al decidir si un caso amerita derivacion no urgente, derivacion urgente, o manejo en atencion primaria (Referral Engine Specification, ICIS-REF-001, Seccion 4.1).

El riesgo racial nunca decide una derivacion por si solo -- funciona exclusivamente como modificador de probabilidad previa (Seccion 5.5, Predisposition Model), en conjunto con hallazgos objetivos del caso y el resultado de CC-262 (Red Flag Matching Engine). Un breed con riesgo Very High documentado para una condicion de un sistema dado no genera, por si mismo, una recomendacion de derivacion -- eleva la sensibilidad con la que CC-260 evalua hallazgos compatibles con esa condicion en ese paciente especifico.

Cuando el sistema clinicamente comprometido en el caso actual corresponda a un bloque de inteligencia por sistema que todavia no esta poblado en ICIS-KD-001 (ver nota de alcance en el Capitulo 4.12), CC-260 debe operar sin el modificador racial para ese sistema y declararlo explicitamente como limitacion de la evaluacion (AI Reasoning Standard, Seccion 2, Area de Incertidumbre) -- nunca debe inferirse un nivel de riesgo por analogia con otro sistema o especie.

El Organ-Specific Risk Profile (Seccion 9.9) es la estructura de datos que CC-260 y CC-261 consultan para este proposito una vez que un sistema tenga su bloque de inteligencia poblado.

Chapter 7 — Breed Knowledge Acquisition & Curation Framework

7.1 Purpose

The Breed Knowledge Acquisition & Curation Framework (BKACF) defines the governance model for acquiring, validating, curating, publishing, maintaining and retiring breed-specific knowledge within the Internal Medicine Clinical Intelligence System (ICIS).

Its objective is to ensure that every knowledge element incorporated into the Breed Intelligence Knowledge Base is scientifically valid, clinically relevant, traceable and continuously maintained throughout its lifecycle.

The framework establishes a repeatable process for transforming scientific evidence into structured clinical knowledge that can be safely consumed by AI engines.

7.2 Guiding Principles

The framework is governed by the following principles:

Scientific Integrity

Every knowledge object shall originate from verifiable scientific evidence.

Clinical Relevance

Only information with potential impact on clinical decision-making shall be incorporated.

Traceability

Every assertion shall be linked to its original evidence source.

Explainability

Every knowledge element shall include sufficient metadata to explain how and why it is used by AI.

Continuous Evolution

Knowledge is considered dynamic and shall evolve as new evidence becomes available.

Human Oversight

No AI-generated knowledge shall be published without review by qualified veterinary experts.

Every Breed Knowledge Object shall follow the lifecycle below.

Scientific Discovery

↓

↓

↓

↓

Normalization

↓

↓

Scientific Validation

↓

Committee Approval

↓

Publication

↓

AI Consumption

↓

Clinical Feedback

↓

Periodic Review

↓

Revision

Knowledge shall never bypass this lifecycle.

The framework recognizes the following evidence sources.

Tier 1 — Primary Scientific Evidence

-   Systematic Reviews

-   Meta-Analyses

-   Randomized Clinical Trials

-   Controlled Clinical Studies

Highest priority.

-   WSAVA Global Guidelines

-   BMC Veterinary Research

-   Jubb, Kennedy & Palmer's Pathology

-   FCI

-   Internal consistency

Chapter 9 — Breed Risk Classification Model

9.1 Purpose

The Breed Risk Classification Model (BRCM) defines the standardized methodology used to quantify, classify and communicate breed-associated clinical risk within the Internal Medicine Clinical Intelligence System.

Its objective is to transform qualitative observations (e.g., "predisposed") into structured, evidence-based quantitative risk assessments that can be interpreted consistently by clinicians and AI engines.

The model shall distinguish between predisposition, probability, severity and clinical impact.

9.2 Design Principles

The Breed Risk Classification Model shall:

-   Be evidence-driven.

-   Be transparent and explainable.

-   Support Bayesian reasoning.

-   Be disease-independent.

-   Allow future recalibration.

-   Never replace patient-specific evidence.

9.3 Risk Dimensions

Risk shall not be represented by a single value.

Each Breed Knowledge Object shall evaluate multiple independent dimensions.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Dimension                   Purpose
  --------------------------- -------------------------------------------
  Disease Susceptibility      Likelihood of developing a disease

  Genetic Risk                Presence of inherited predisposition

  Age Risk                    Age-related susceptibility

  Sex Risk                    Influence of biological sex

  Severity Risk               Expected disease severity

  Progression Risk            Probability of progression

  Recurrence Risk             Probability of relapse

  Mortality Risk              Disease-related mortality

  Treatment Complexity        Expected management complexity

  Prognostic Risk             Expected long-term outcome
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

9.4 Risk Classification Scale

All risks shall be expressed using a standardized five-level scale.

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Level   Classification   Interpretation
  ------- ---------------- ---------------------------------------------------------
  R1      Minimal          No meaningful increase over baseline population

  R2      Low              Slightly increased risk

  R3      Moderate         Clinically relevant increase

  R4      High             Strong evidence of significant predisposition

  R5      Very High        Exceptional predisposition supported by robust evidence
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This categorical scale shall coexist with quantitative metrics.

9.5 Relative Risk Model

Whenever available, risk shall include numerical values.

Example:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Disease                                           Relative Risk
  ------------------------------------------------- ---------------------
  Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency                 8.4

  Chronic Enteropathy                               2.7

  Pancreatitis                                      1.8
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Relative Risk values shall always be accompanied by:

-   Confidence Interval

-   Evidence Level

-   Sample Size

-   Source Reference

9.6 Absolute Risk

Where epidemiological data exists, the model shall also represent absolute risk.

Example:

Breed:

German Shepherd

Disease:

Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency

Lifetime Absolute Risk:

5.2%

Relative Risk:

8.4

Risk Category:

R5

Absolute risk provides context that relative risk alone cannot.

9.7 Risk Confidence Score

Every risk estimate shall include a confidence assessment.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Confidence          Interpretation
  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------------
  Very Low            Sparse or conflicting evidence

  Low                 Limited observational evidence

  Moderate            Consistent observational evidence

  High                Multiple high-quality studies

  Very High           Guideline-supported consensus
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

This confidence score reflects the quality of the evidence, not the certainty that an individual patient has the disease.

9.8 Composite Breed Risk Index (CBRI)

To provide a concise overview, every Breed Clinical Profile shall calculate a Composite Breed Risk Index.

The index integrates:

-   Disease susceptibility

-   Genetic contribution

-   Severity

-   Frequency

-   Evidence quality

Example:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Breed                                            CBRI
  ------------------------------------------------ ----------------------
  German Shepherd                                  87 / 100

  Labrador Retriever                               58 / 100

  Boxer                                            71 / 100
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The CBRI is intended for comparative visualization and prioritization, not for diagnosis.

9.9 Organ-Specific Risk Profiles

Risk shall be assessed independently for each organ system.

Example:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Organ System                                          Risk
  ----------------------------------------------------- -----------------
  Esophagus                                             R2

  Stomach                                               R3

  Small Intestine                                       R5

  Colon                                                 R3

  Liver                                                 R2

  Gallbladder                                           R1

  Pancreas                                              R5

  Rectum                                                R2
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

This enables focused reasoning by subsystem.

9.10 Disease-Specific Risk Matrix

Each Breed Clinical Profile shall maintain a structured disease matrix.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Disease                                Risk   Evidence    Confidence
  -------------------------------------- ------ ----------- --------------
  Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency      R5     Level I     Very High

  Chronic Enteropathy                    R4     Level II    High

  Pancreatitis                           R3     Level III   Moderate
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

The matrix shall be directly consumable by the Diagnostic Correlation Engine.

9.11 Risk Modifiers

Baseline breed risk may be modified by contextual factors.

Examples include:

-   Age

-   Sex

-   Neuter status

-   Body Condition Score

-   Diet

-   Geographic region

-   Previous gastrointestinal disease

-   Environmental exposures

Risk modifiers shall be explicitly documented and never applied implicitly.

9.12 Protective Factors

The model shall also record factors associated with decreased disease likelihood.

Examples:

-   Documented resistance to specific diseases.

-   Lower-than-average incidence.

-   Favorable therapeutic response.

-   Protective genetic variants.

Protective factors reduce prior probability but do not exclude disease.

9.13 Dynamic Risk Adjustment

Risk shall evolve as patient-specific information becomes available.

Illustrative workflow:

Breed Prior

↓

Signalment

↓

History

↓

Physical Examination

↓

Laboratory Findings

↓

Imaging

↓

Endoscopy

↓

Histopathology

↓

Posterior Probability

Breed risk represents only the initial context.

-   Radial Risk Charts

-   Organ Heat Maps

-   Comparative Risk Bars

-   Timeline Risk Evolution

-   Confidence Indicators

-   Evidence Badges

Visual encodings shall remain consistent across all modules.

9.15 Explainability Requirements

Whenever breed risk influences reasoning, the AI shall explain:

-   Which risk category was applied.

-   Which evidence supports the classification.

-   How much the prior probability changed.

-   Which subsequent findings increased or decreased confidence.

Users shall always distinguish between baseline risk and patient-specific evidence.

9.16 Governance

Risk classifications shall be reviewed whenever:

-   New high-quality evidence is published.

-   Consensus guidelines change.

-   Significant epidemiological shifts are identified.

-   Internal validation demonstrates systematic over- or underestimation.

Historical classifications shall remain archived for auditability.

9.18 AI Prompt Contract

When evaluating breed-associated risk, the AI shall:

1.  Retrieve the published risk profile for the identified breed.

2.  Apply only evidence-supported risk categories.

3.  Incorporate approved contextual modifiers.

4.  Recalculate posterior probabilities as new evidence becomes available.

5.  Present both categorical and quantitative risk estimates when available.

6.  Clearly explain the influence of breed risk on diagnostic prioritization.

7.  Never diagnose disease solely because a breed belongs to a high-risk category.
